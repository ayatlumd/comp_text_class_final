china of "keeping proceedings closed to the public" and, "in some cases, even family members in sensitive cases." it also gave an example that "pastor xu yongze's family received no notification of his mid-1997 trial." the fact is that xu yongze was detained on may 21, 1997. the public security department notified his daughter, who works in the nanyang department store, of this by express mail on may 22. after xu was arrested on june 25, the public security department notified his wife by express mail, too. the court announced his formal trial three days in advance. therefore, there did not exist the issue of blocking access to information for xu's family and lawyer as the report alleged. the report also played up the case of xu yongze, stating that xu was "sentenced to ten years of reform through labor" because he conducted normal missionary activities, and used this as evidence for china's religious persecution. the fact is that in 1984 xu yongze set up a cult named the "all-round church." he asserted wantonly among people: "the end of the world is imminent, so are disasters" and that while gathering "people must weep loudly. this is the only way to reincarnate and save themselves". instigated by him, many people abandoned their work, production and study, and threw themselves into an orgy of weeping in groups daily. as a result, they lost means to support themselves, became mentally ill and could not take care of themselves. some people went blind from weeping and some even committed suicide. this cult seriously disturbed production and lives of local people, and disrupted a normal social order, causing grave damages to society and the people.
xu was sentenced to three years of reform through labor in beijing in 1988. after he served his sentence and was released in april 1991, xu did not repent, but, on the contrary, intensified his efforts to engage in socially disruptive activities. in june 1997, the public security department of zhengzhou city in henan province arrested xu in accordance with the law. in december the local court sentenced xu to three years' imprisonment for the crime of disrupting social order. the fact is that xu yongze was sentenced for breaking the law. but the report alleged that he was sentenced because he conducted normal religious activities. it is a total distortion of the facts.  the report claimed that "the migration of ethnic han into xinjiang in recent decades has caused the han-uyghur ratio in the capital of urumqi to shift from 20:80 to 80:20." however, according to statistics, the total population of urumqi was 635,000 in 1960. the ratio of the populations of the han and minority nationalities was 75.2:24.8. in 1996 the population in the city increased to 1.479 million. of this number, 1.076 million were han and 406,000 belonged to minority nationalities --a ratio of 72.8:27.2. it is obvious that the fact that the population of the han nationality is relatively larger in the city is due to historical reasons. in fact, the growth rate of the han population has been far below the that of the population of minority nationalities in the city in the past few decades. in fact, the ratio of han people to the population has actually decreased.  there is great discrepancy between the figures in the report and facts.
the report also attacked china for discriminating against tibetans in tibet. it said, "overall, government development policies have helped raise the economic living standards of many ethnic tibetans, but most benefits of development accrue to han chinese." the fact that the living standards of many tibetans have been raised means that many tibetans have benefited from such development. how can the report say that most benefits accrue to the han people? as a matter of fact, the 62 projects undertaken by the chinese government in the 1990s for tibet aimed at bringing a happy life to the tibetan people.  according to statistics, by 1996, 56 projects of the 62 had been completed, which greatly improved tibet's infrastructure, including transportation, energy and telecommunications. they directly benefited more than one million people.  in the tibet autonomous region the population of tibetans accounts for more than 90 percent of the total population.  obviously, those who have benefited are mainly tibetans.  meanwhile, the han people in tibet have also naturally benefited from the projects. it is totally groundless for the report to claim that "most benefits accrue to the han people." the contents of the report confused right and wrong. for example, the report blamed the chinese government for its poverty-alleviation policy, saying that this program through resettlement "forces the evacuation of persons from the poorest mountain areas" and "disrupts the traditional living patterns of minority groups".  to solve the problem of insufficient food and clothing of people in some regions, the chinese government has formulated and implemented a key poverty-alleviation program which aims to help 80 million people to get rid of poverty within seven years (1994-2000). the program includes a plan for helping the people living in extremely harsh natural conditions to cast off poverty through resettlement. the plan represents a pioneering undertaking of the chinese people in their fight against the harsh natural environment.  facts have proved that the plan is welcomed by the local people, for it is an effective measure adopted by the government to help the people in the poverty-stricken regions to improve their human rights conditions.  take shangxing village of tianyang county in a mountainous region in the guangxi zhuang autonomous region as an example. the village is characterized by low-lying land, with little water available within a circumference of dozens of kilometers and rocks all around. for a long time villagers lived on the maize that they planted in crevices between the rocks.  the arable land per capita of the villagers was only about 0.01 ha, and the annual income per capita, 160 yuan. every year the villagers used to be short of food for five or six months. after they were re-settled in 1995, the annual income of the villagers per capita reached 1,360 yuan, an increase by 9.5 times.  their living conditions have been greatly improved and the villagers have supported the resettlement. the poverty relief measure through resettlement similar to this is really one beneficial to the people and to the improvement of human rights. but the report cited it as proof for infringment of human rights
two. the report reveals political bias of the writer on china different from previous years' versions, this year's report mentioned a number of times the "positive progress" china has made in human rights, and even recognized in some place that "average citizens go about their daily lives with more personal freedom than ever before." however, in its overall assessment of human rights in china, the report asserted that there is still "widespread and well-documented human rights abuses" in china. this evidently revealed the fact that the judgement of the writer of the report on china's human rights is not based on facts but on intrinsic political bias.  it is widely recognized that 1997 is the year that witnessed remarkable progress in china's human rights cause. during the year, china achieved favorable national economic development with a high growth rate and a low inflation rate, people's living standard rose steadily and the country's human rights situation further improved.  statistics show the gdp calculated in constant price increased by 8.8 percent in 1997 from the previous year. the per capita disposable income of urban residents reached 5,140 yuan, a rise of 2.9 percent with the price factor deducted. the per capita net income of rural residents was 2,080 yuan, up 4 percent in real terms.  the number of people under the poverty line decreased to 50 million from 1978's 250 million. in 1997, another 400 counties (cities and districts), which account for 15 percent of the total population, basically realized nine-year compulsory education and eliminated illiteracy among the middle aged and the young. thus, a total areas with 65 percent of the national population have met the above requirement. four million young and and middle-aged people became literate last year, enabling the areas with 73 percent of the national population to basically eliminate illiteracy in the above mentioned age group.  the promotion of democracy and the establishment of a legal system were stepped up evidently. the citizen and political rights were effectively safeguarded. statistics show between the beginning of the reform and opening up program and november of 1997, the national people's congress (npc) and its standing committee enacted 324 laws, 114 or a third of which were adopted by the eighth national people's congress and its standing committee.  meanwhile, the state council formulated 780 sets of rules and regulations. local people's congresses formulated a total of 5,300 sets of local rules and regulations. as a result, a comprehensive legal system that safeguards human rights has been introduced.  the 15th cpc national congress, in particular, clearly set forth the principle that "the governing of country by the communist party of china means respecting and safeguarding human rights," and included in china's cross-century development strategy the enhancement of human rights and the administration of the country in accordance with law as an important objective in the reform of the political structure and the promotion of democracy and the introduction of a legal system.  this has brought about bright prospects for the development of china's human rights cause. china is now enjoying political stability, economic growth and ethnic unity. its people are living and working in peace and contentment. the human rights conditions are showing a strong tendency toward all-round improvement.  however, the report ignored the aforementioned objective facts and called on absurd logic to accuse china of "widespread human rights abuses." the logic is whatever permits the opposition to china's legitimate government and to the status of the cpc as the ruling party conforms to human rights. otherwise, it means abusing human rights.

the report attacked china for serious human rights abuses. in the final analysis, that is because china does not tolerate the right to overthrow its legitimate government.  for instance, the report said that china infringes upon its citizens' freedom of information, producing as evidence the fact that china bans using the internet to "incite the overthrow of the government or the socialist system or "incite division of the country and harming national unification." the report made an accusation that there is no freedom of speech or freedom of the press in china, claiming that china "stipulates that people cannot publish any material that is contrary to the constitution, reveals state secrets, endangers national security, or harms the country." the report said that china restricts freedom of peaceful assembly and association on the grounds that china's constitution provides that such activities may not challenge party leadership or infringe upon the interests of the state." the report accused china of restricting freedom of religious belief in tibet, claiming that because "the chinese government does not tolerate religious manifestations that advocate tibetan independence or any expression of splittism." all these accusations boil down to demanding that china should give a tiny number of law-breakers and criminals the freedom to incite the overthrow of the government, the cpc leadership and the socialist system.  this is tantamount to saying that whatever activities that help to achieve this purpose, even if they are law-breaking activities regarded as crimes around the world, such as "violating the constitution, revealing state secrets and endangering state security", "inciting splittism or harming national unity," should not be prohibited because they all "conform to human rights." when china punishes such activities according to law, some people in the united states cry out that china "suppresses dissidents" and "infringes upon human rights on a large scale". that is their logic. prejudice is farther away from truth than ignorance.  it entirely conforms to international norms for china to restrain citizens' freedom of information, speech, publication, assembly, association and religious belief.  the international convention on civil and political rights of the united nations clearly states in its articles 18 to 22 that such freedoms should not endanger state security, public security, public order, public sanitation or ethics, or the rights and freedoms of others.  no country will allow its citizens to abuse their freedoms and rights to engage in criminal activities such as violating the constitution, inciting people to overthrow the government and the social system, endangering state security and unification, revealing state secrets and harming state interests.  in articles 2381 to 2391 of chapter 18, the legal code of the united states of america stipulates severe punishment measures for such activities.  the report cited china's stipulations and measures, which conform to international norms, as evidence of china's "human rights abuses." in fact, the real purpose of the writers of the report is to, under the pretext of human rights, support a handful of anti-government elements in china in disrupting china's stability, and eventually overthrowing china's legitimate government and changing china's road of development.
three. the report revealed the united states' efforts in using human rights diplomacy to enforce power politics.  the report portrayed the united states itself as a universal embodiment of human rights, attacking china repeatedly for going against "internationally accepted norms", censuring china for making itself an "exception to the principle of universality" of the "universal declaration of human rights".  in fact, it is the united states which takes itself as an "exception to the principle of universality" of the "universal declaration of human rights". one of the most important contributions the "declaration" has made to the world is that it not only for the first time on a global basis affirmed the citizen's and political rights of the people, but also their economic, social, and cultural rights.  the intact connotation of the universal principle of the "declaration" rests on the fact that not only do citizen's and political rights have universalities, but so do economic, social and cultural rights.  the united states, however, which has consistently regarded itself as a spokesman for this universality, has openly taken itself as an exception to the principle of universality by not acknowledging until the present date the economic, social and cultural rights as parts of the human rights in its constitution.  as to the human rights diplomacy adopted by the united states, it is a further demonstration of the country's exception to the universality of human rights the "country reports on human rights practices" issued by the state department of the united states each year focus on 190-plus nations and regions around the world, but the report takes exception to the united states by mentioning not even a word on the human rights record of its own.  as an american scholar pointed out, such human rights standard is an example of the country's practice to style itself as an exception to the universality of human rights...regarding itself as an example for other countries to follow, thus making the so-called international standard of human rights meaningless.
china has always respected the "declaration of human rights" and the principle of the universality of human rights. china has actively participated in and strictly complied with international conventions on human rights adopted by the united nations and all other related international documents.  in addition, the fact that china has adopted effective measures to safeguard and promote human rights in accordance with the law and reality within the country clearly shows the integration of the universalities and specialities of human rights.  like many other countries in the world, china can not act in line with the human rights standard set by the united states. conditions and realities in china are, in fact, quite different than those in the united states.  the united states has invaded many countries over the past 200-odd years, but has itself remained free of foreign incursions. china, on the other hand, has suffered from the humiliation of being carved up by foreign aggressors and has experienced the tribulations of long-time wars, as well as massive losses of life over the past 100 years and more.  china treasures its hard-won national independence, sovereignty, and national dignity, while at the same time cherishing a stable and peaceful environment.  the differing realities between china and the united states are quite obvious. for example, china's population is 4.6 times that of the united states, with per capita arable land in the former representing only one-tenth that of the latter. in addition china's per capita share of grain output accounts for a mere 22 percent of the share for the united states, and its per capita portions of other resources are far less than those of the latter.  china is a large developing country with a backward economy, while the united states is a superpower. china's economic development level is far below that of the united states, with its per capita share of wealth accounting for only 1.6 percent of that in the united states.  in sharp contrast to the united states, development is the top priority in china and represents the key to solving a wide assortment of problems, but stable social and political environments constitute the preconditions for china's development.
china's top priority in fostering human rights centers on ensuring the rights to existence and development, as well as promoting a comprehensive progress in human rights through development under stable conditions. this is the road china should follow in developing its human rights.  china has adopted a social system quite different from that of the united states. the fact that china had inherited its independent cultural traditions that could trace back to one origin and date back over 5,000 years has quite naturally resulted in differing views of values when compared to the united states.  unlike the united states in terms of views of values which respect and emphasize individual rights, china places greater stress on the coordination of rights and obligations, individuals and all other people, collectives and the society as a whole.  china places greater emphasis on values which require placing the interests of others and collectives before oneself. it is ludicrous to expect china to adopt u.s. standards and management models given the vast differences in the histories, realities and cultures of the two nations.  as a matter of fact, both the specialities and universalities of human rights involve objective existence, and there are indeed specialities in human rights in the united states.  the united states is home to less than five percent of the world's total population, nonetheless accounts for 30 percent of the world's petroleum, natural gas, and coal consumption. china, with a population of over 1.2 billion, would have surpassed the world's total consumption figure by 140 percent if all chinese citizens consumed as much as the average american. that is to say, the global consumption would be not enough for the consumption of china alone. is it possible to imagine what would have happened to the world would that be the case?  the world would have fallen into chaos had americans been allowed to promoted such excess privileges to the world as the universality of human rights.  the united states is simply a member of the world community and is in no position to censure human rights of other countries. in the world today, the united states is the only country which attempts to act as an "international judge on human rights" by overriding the sovereignty of other nations and releasing annual reports on human rights situation in other countries in the name of u.s. government.  united states' such actions have severely violated the international norms on equality and sovereignty of all nations and on noninterference in the internal affairs of another country. said actions totally defy the goals and principles of the "united nations charter".  the human rights report issued by the u.s state department is nothing more than a product of u.s. internal laws in accordance with the "foreign assistance act" enacted by the united states congress in 1974 and the "hartin amendment" in 1979.  the united states not only lacks authority from the united nations or other accepted international organizations, but also lacks the backing of international laws to make judgements on human rights situations in other countries based on its domestic laws. said illegal practices to make judgements at random on the human rights situation in other countries represent nothing but hegemonism and power politics.
the united states is by no means qualified to preach to china.  over the past 100 years, the united states has many times imposed aggressive wars and unequal treaties on china, supporting the kuomintang reactionaries in china's civil war, launching the korean war in an attempt to strangle new china, bringing about heavy losses to the life and property of the chinese people, and infringing upon human rights on a large scale in china. in the united states itself there exist severe human rights problems.  many of the accusations against china in the report could be used to satirize the united states. when the united states attacks china for discriminating against minority ethnic groups, it should remember its history of genocide of native indians and enslavement of kidnapped black people. racial discrimination is still rampant in the us even today.  according to statistics released in 1977, the average life expectancies of black men and women in the united states were 5.4 years and 7.5 years lower than those of white men and women.  in comparison, china does much better than the united states in practicing ethnic equality, giving favorable treatment to minority ethnic groups and protecting the human rights of the people of such groups.  although the united states is the richest country in the world, 20 percent of its population live below poverty line. china is not prosperous, but by its own efforts it has succeeded in basically providing adequate food and clothing for 1.2 billion people. the number of people living below the poverty line has decreased by 200 million in less than 20 years.  the french weekly le nouvel observateur reported in 1997 that the average working hours of american workers increased from 40.6 per week in 1973 to 50.6 hours in 1995.  according to a us national commission in charge of equal pay for equal work, an ordinary american woman earns 500,000 dollars less than an ordinary male employee in her lifetime.  in comparison, the weekly working time of chinese workers has been cut from 48 hours to 40 hours, according to law, and men and women get equal pay for equal work.  the united states accuses china's judicial system of not safeguarding human rights. but both the rate of violent crimes and the proportion of prisoners in the united states are the highest in the world, and the recidivism rate in the us is more than 40 percent. these figures are far higher than those in china.  the united states accuses china of not respecting international human rights norms, but the united states has joined fewer international human rights conventions than any other major country. so far, the united states has joined only 15 international conventions on human rights. china has joined 17. the united states should first look at its domestic human rights problems.
it is well known that the human rights report periodically released by the us state department resulted from the political needs of the cold war launched by the united states.  from the very beginning it has served as a political weapon of the us government to contain socialist countries, put pressure on developing countries and practise hegemonism.  with the end of the cold war and the development of multi-polarization, people all over the world are demanding an end to cold war thinking and confrontation. they seek equal dialogue and co-operation so as to promote the healthy development of human rights around the world.  at present when proposing dialogue and opposing confrontation have become the irresistible historical trend, the united states has already received strong opposition and resistance from nations across the globe for clinging to a relic of the cold war --the human rights report.  for too many years, the report has been a giant barrier for developing the sino-us relationship.  chinese president jiang zemin's successful visit to the us last year ushered in a new phase of development for the relations between the two countries.  the two countries issued a "joint statement", which dedicated them to building a strategic partnership.  although the two sides admitted that significant differences exist in dealing with the human rights issue, they have agreed to solve the problem through governmental and non-governmental means in the spirit of equality and mutual respect, which reflects the requirements of the times and the aspirations of the people.  it is the hope that the us state department would change over to new ways and return to the correct road of dialogue and co-operation in dealing with the human rights issue.
