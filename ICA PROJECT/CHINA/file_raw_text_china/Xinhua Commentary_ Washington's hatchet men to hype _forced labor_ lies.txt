by Xinhua writer Dong Yue BEIJING, July 7 (Xinhua) -- Some U.S. think-tanks that pretend to be independent and objective have long served as the right-hand man of an increasingly overbearing Washington in advancing its self-serving political agendas. Their latest collusion seeks to hype up again alleged "forced labor" in China's Xinjiang. After the United States' so-called "Uyghur Forced Labor Prevention Act" came into effect, those research bodies quickly followed up by publishing their Xinjiang-related "findings" to help support Washington's groundless accusations, further revealing those institutes' deplorable nature as Washington's accomplice in containing China. A typical view of such illogical China-bashing rhetoric alleged that global companies could be unknowingly supporting "forced labor" in Xinjiang by importing goods from Chinese companies headquartered in other parts of China. 
All these "research results" are based on the so-called "forced labor" in Xinjiang, a fictitious premise, a staggering lie and an insidious scheme. Xinjiang cotton has been a major target of this smear campaign. Thanks to the high degree of mechanization of the cotton industry in Xinjiang, there is no need for too much manpower, not to mention "forced labor." In 2021, the mechanization rate of cotton-picking in the region exceeded 80 percent. As for cotton sowing, the comprehensive mechanization rate in most areas of the region exceeds 98 percent. Meanwhile, Chinese law categorically prohibits forced labor. As a matter of fact, people of all ethnicities in Xinjiang enjoy complete freedom and equality in labor and employment. Their rights to work are effectively protected in accordance with the law and their living standards have seen a constant improvement. From 2014 to 2021, the disposable income of urban residents in the region increased from 23,000 yuan (about 3,430 U.S. dollars) to 37,600 yuan (about 5,600 dollars), and that of rural residents rose from approximately 8,700 yuan (about 1,300 dollars) to 15,600 yuan (about 2,330 dollars). By the end of 2020, over 3.06 million people in Xinjiang had been lifted out of poverty. The hype of so-called "forced labor" in Xinjiang is a total contempt of international law. The International Labour Organization's Forced Labour Convention from 1930 has clearly defined "forced labor." But U.S. politicians and think-tanks have maliciously misinterpreted and abused the concept as a weapon to slander others. As Lawrence Wilkerson, chief of staff to former U.S. Secretary of State Colin Powell, once publicly confessed, the best way for the U.S. Central Intelligence Agency to destabilize China would be "to foment unrest" in Xinjiang. So-called "forced labor" provides an excuse for Washington to achieve that aim. The United States' other despicable purpose of spreading the lie of "forced labor" in Xinjiang is to exclude China from the global supply chains. China is the world's largest photovoltaic (PV) manufacturer. It has more installed capacity than any other country. Xinjiang is home to the world's most important production base of polysilicon, an essential material of the PV industry. By fabricating such a lie, the United States aims to achieve its sinister goal of suppressing the competitive edge of Xinjiang's PV industry and relocate it out of China through political manipulation. Moreover, speaking of forced labor, the United States should have a look at its own history and reality. It still has not ratified the Forced Labour Convention from 1930, the United Nations Convention on the Rights of the Child and the Convention on the Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination against Women. Every year, nearly 100,000 people are smuggled into the United States for forced labor. Today, there are at least half a million people enslaved in the country. Washington's manipulation of the issue of "forced labor" to suppress China will only backfire, and hurt the world at large. The move has disrupted the international trade order and seriously endangered the security of the global supply chains, which are already fragile and vulnerable. Washington and its pawns, especially those think-tanks, must be aware that the Xinjiang-related lies they have concocted will not undermine the stability, unity, harmonious development in Xinjiang or silence the just voices of the international community. And if they truly want to do something to tackle forced labor in the world, perhaps they should start by engaging in some serious self-examination. Enditem
