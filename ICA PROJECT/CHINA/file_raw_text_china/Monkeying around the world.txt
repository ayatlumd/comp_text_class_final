The Chinese New Year is one of the most important festivals of the year for Chinese. A time for families to reunite and spread good wishes for the coming new year, Chinese New Year is celebrated by Chinese all over the world and is now even being embraced by other cultures due to its deep cultural meaning and cheerful atmosphere. Washington DC, New York, Oakland, Sydney, St Petersburg and Almaty, cities around the globe celebrate the holiday in their own unique ways. Washington DC, USThis year the Kennedy Center was decorated in red, a lucky color in Chinese culture, on February 6. Chinese knots and lanterns were hung everywhere around the facility as part of the center's "Chinese New Year: Family Day" activity open to kids of all ages. 
Some 6,500 people took part in the event that day. Kids were provided free classes on Chinese paper cutting and calligraphy, as well as given demonstrations of Peking Opera and lantern making. Monkey King mask decorating was another highlight celebrating The Year of the Monkey. This was not the only activity the Kennedy Center held for Chinese New Year. On February 5, Jam You, a Shenzhen-based music band, performed in front of some 800 people, while the Shenzhen Lily Girls' Choir and Henan Arts Troupe brought traditional Chinese songs and operas on February 7 and 8 respectively. New York City, USOn February 9, the second day of the Chinese New Year, a free public performance hosted by the Lincoln Center was held in Lincoln Square. During the performance, the National Dance Institute together with a local Chinese dance club performed the traditional Lion and Dragon Dance for the public. On the night of the same day, the Lincoln Center held a special concert for the Chinese New Year during which the New York Philharmonic performed a series of Chinese classic pieces, such as "The Spring Festival Overture," "The Butterfly Lovers" and "Nu Shu: The Secret Songs of Women." A special guest for the performance, Ban Ki-moon, the current secretary-general of the UN, greeted the audience and gave a speech before the show. Calling the monkey a representative of passion, creativity, wisdom and intellegence, Ban wished the audience a happy and prosperous year. "What a fantastic idea to celebrate the New Year of 2016 with this beautiful musical harmony," said Ban, as quoted by the Xinhua News Agency. Oakland, USOn the night of February 9, the Oracle Arena hosted a basketball game between the Golden State Warriors and the Houston Rockets. However, unlike other games, the Golden State Warriors all wore jerseys featuring the Chinese characters for yongshi (Warrior) and an image of a monkey. Introductions for the players on the big screen were in Chinese, and during halftime, cheerleaders dressed in Chinese qipao in the representative colors of the Warriors - gold and blue took the court. What pushed the Chinese Spring Festival atmosphere to a high was the Lion and Dragon Dance performed by a club from San Francisco during halftime.St Petersburg, Russia In St Petersburg, a 17-day long special festival featuring Chinese culture is currently being held February 7-23. The festival includes a Chinese film week, various cultural master classes, concerts, exhibitions, Lantern Festival temple fairs and Chinese chess competitions. At a press conference on February 8, students from local martial arts schools dressed up like the Monkey King and Chinese students in Russia put on traditional Chinese clothing. Almaty, KazakhstanOn February 9, a song and dance ensemble from China's Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region performed for nearly 1,000 people, including local government officials, locals and visiting Chinese. Ethnic Uyghur, Han and Kazak songs and dances demonstrated the ethnic and cultural variety of China.Sydney, Australia For the Chinese New Year, the Sydney Opera House and Sydney Harbor Bridge were lit up in auspicious red for the first time ever. Sydney has possibly one of the largest celebrations of the Chinese New Year held outside China. The city will have held more than 80 activities by February 21. A highlight of the celebration are the 12 giant Chinese zodiac lanterns that have been raised at famous landmarks around the city. For example, an 8-meter high monkey lantern is currently lighting up the Sydney Opera House, while an ox lantern made from 700 mahjong tiles graces Martin Place. Meanwhile a tiger lantern sits in front of the Queen Victoria Building and 14 rabbit lanterns practicing tai chi stand in the square outside the Customs House.All these lanterns were made by Australian Chinese artists: Fan Dongwang, Zu Tianli, Hu Ming, Claudia Chan Shaw, Laurens Tan and Justin Qian.
