Sotheby's 2013 autumn auction in Beijing garners 227 million yuan. Photo: CFP
Last week, the Chinese mainland's autumn auction season neared its end as several local auction heavyweights, including the Poly International Auction and the Council Auction Company, dropped their gavels. With high prices in leading categories such as contemporary Chinese art and oil painting, as well as generally high turnover in special auctions, signs of a strong rebound for the auction market are further confirmed.Paintings, paintingsAs always, contemporary Chinese art is the focus of every auction season in China. On December 2, Jubilant Grasslands by Huang Zhou (1925-97) sold for 128.8 million yuan ($21 million) at Poly's auction, the highest price of the year for paintings on the Chinese mainland.This masterpiece, created in 1981, shows the Kirgiz people of the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region engaging in their traditional sport of "wrestling on horseback." The painting depicts seven women, nine shepherd dogs and more than 70 horses, reflecting the artist's high competence in both drawing figures and animals.Actually, Huang had produced two such paintings of the same size but with slight variations in color and character detail. 
One now hangs in the No.18 Building of the Diaoyutai State Guesthouse for the reception of visiting foreign heads of state. The auctioned piece had been gifted by Chinese leaders in 1984 to the visiting American entrepreneur Armand Hammer, whose grandson now returned the piece for auction.This year's auctions featured a spell of Huang Zhou fever, as several of his other paintings also appeared and sold for good prices. At Council's auction of Chinese calligraphy and paintings on December 5, Huang's The Patrol Scene (1962) sold for 45.42 million yuan, the highest of that auction. Another two of his paintings, The Happy Generation (1972) and All the Beauty (1984), were well-received by the market as well.The oil painting category also had its highlights. In China Guardian's November 16 auction, Tajik Bride by Jin Shangyi sold for 85.1 million yuan - the highest price ever for the company in 20 years of oil painting auctions.At Sotheby's auction in Beijing on December 1, Abstraction by French-Chinese artist Zao Wou-Ki (1921-2013), sold for 89.68 million yuan, more than double the estimated sale price of 35 million yuan - and the highest price ever for the abstractionist master of oil painting.Poly's contemporary art auction showed a total volume of 289 million yuan for its 80 pieces, with the turnover rate at 78.32 percent. Meanwhile, Council's oil painting category garnered 140 million yuan. "The recovery of the auction market is strongly felt this season, as buyers have ceased with their 'wait and see' attitude, and begun to act," said Xie Xiaodong, vice general manager of Council. "The signs are clearer in the leading category of contemporary art and oil paintings," he told the Global Times.Threatening foreign magnatesAdding to the bustle of this year's mainland auctions was the participation of foreign auction tycoons such as Sotheby's and Christie's. A little over a year after Sotheby's Auction Co. Ltd. (Beijing) was established, they held their first large-scale auction in Beijing on December 1.Out of a total 144 pieces, 112 contemporary Chinese artworks were successfully auctioned, with the turnover rate at 80 percent and the total volume at 227 million yuan. Moreover, before the autumn auction, Sotheby's hosted a series of art events including auctions, private purchases, exhibitions and lectures. "Sotheby's Beijing Art Week," from November 28 to December 1, covered a wide range of contemporary Chinese art, with only seven foreign artworks.Introducing an art week during the auction season is still a fresh phenomenon for mainland collectors. Christie's had tried the tactic during its debut in Shanghai this September after becoming the first foreign auction house to acquire the license to auction independently in the Chinese mainland in April (Sotheby's Beijing is a joint venture). Though smaller in scale with only 40 lots, the turnover rate for Christie's auction was 98 percent, with total volume at 154 million yuan.The two tycoons are experimenting with targeted strategies in approaching the mainland market. For Christie's, bringing Western fine art and introducing Western masters like Picasso and Morandi to Chinese collectors appears to be a shortcut to winning the attention of local customers. Sotheby's seems to have adopted a more local approach, as reflected in their large number of contemporary Chinese art offerings. "While others like coffee, we like tea," said Kevin Ching, CEO of Sotheby's Asia."Currently constrained by policies restricting foreign companies from auctioning Chinese cultural relics, their influence is more on the side of contemporary art," Xie said, by way of analysis. "It's already reflected in this Sotheby's auction in Beijing, where they are more competitive in both collecting those pieces and winning potential buyers."Niche collectionsFan-shaped paintings used to play a minor role within the large category of traditional Chinese paintings. This year, however, they rose to prominence with high turnover rates and prices. At Council's special auction for fan-shaped paintings of the Ming Dynasty (1368-1644), all 50 pieces were auctioned off with an overall value of 26 million yuan."In five years, the price for fan-shaped paintings has risen nearly fivefold," said Dong Guoqiang, president of Council. "Since they are all small-sized, their prices are low. Collectors can own fan-shaped paintings by famed artists like Dong Qichang (1555-1636) and Zhu Zhishan (1460-1527) for less than one million yuan," said Dong.Aged liquors were another standout niche collection this season. At Guardian's auction on December 1, a bottle of Moutai (a state-class liquor brand) produced in 1950 was sold for 2.53 million yuan. During that 2,000-liquor special auction, all Moutai liquors made from 1950s and 1990s were auctioned off, with a total volume of 114 million yuan.The auction of aged liquors in the Chinese mainland began in 2010, when a bottle of Moutai from the 1950s sold for more than one million yuan. As a young category in the auction market, aged liquors have big potential, but the risks are also high. According to insiders, the brand, alcohol content, volatility and flavor type all matter greatly for collectors of aged liquors. <p class='syndigate_disclaimer'>Copyright Global Times.
