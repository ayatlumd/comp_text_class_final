CHINA'S embassy in the US has praised the re-education enforced by Beijing on Uighur Muslim women, saying it has stopped them being "baby-making machines". In comments that have attracted fierce criticism, the embassy's Twitter feed endorsed a link to a Chi nese state media report that claimed the "eradication of extremism" had emancipated Uighur females.
The report, by the pro-government China Daily, cited a study by officials from the Xinjiang Development Research Centre, which has examined the dramatic drop in Uighur birth rates in recent years. The article said the study had found the drop in birth rate was not caused by "forced sterilisation" of the Uighur population, "as repeatedly claimed by some Western scholars and politicians". Instead, it said that thanks to Beijing's education policies, Uighur women had been able to resist "extremist" social pressures for early marriage, unplanned births and large families.
In its tweet, the embassy said: "Study shows that in the process of eradicating extremism, the minds of Uighur women in Xinjiang were emancipated and gender equality and reproductive health were promoted, making them no longer baby-making machines. They are more confident and independent."
Human rights groups claim the drop in birth rates is because Uighur women are being threatened with fines or spells in detention camps if they do not restrict the size of their families. Some women claim to have suffered forced abortion and sterilisation.
The study appears to have been a riposte to the work of Adrian Zenz, a German scholar and senior fellow at
Washington's Victims of Communism Memorial Foundation, who is known for his research on Xinjiang. Using official Chinese data, he released a report which found that between 2015 and 2018 the natural population growth rate in Xinjiang dropped by 84 per cent.
Beijing has disputed his conclusions, attributing the drop in birth rates to improved prosperity, which it says has reduced the need for large families.
Some critics accused the embassy of "tweeting about the jolly perks of locking humans in concentration camps and the lovely mental health benefits of forced sterilisation". Some demanded that Twitter post an official query of the tweet's accuracy.
Mr Zenz also responded via Twitter, writing "You are committing genocide" in a reply to the embassy.
At least a million Uighur men and women are believed to have passed through re-education camps in recent years, which Beijing insists are to educate Uighurs about Islamic extremism.
