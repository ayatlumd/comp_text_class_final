WASHINGTON (AP) — Twitter closed the Chinese embassy account in Washington because of a tweet that Uighur women in Xinjiang Province are emancipated and are no longer "baby-making machines."
Donald Trump's administration, shortly before his term ended, stated that Chinese policies toward minorities like Uighurs in Xinjiang constitute "crimes against humanity" and "genocide."
One of the reasons for this decision was the forced birth control policies imposed by the Chinese authorities on Uighurs, an issue that The Associated Press detailed in an extensive report last year. Another reason—the forced work to which Uighurs are subjected—was also the subject of an Associated Press report on how this policy results in the export of various products to the United States such as computers and cameras.
Twitter stated that the January 7 tweet violated its ban on dehumanizing groups of people.
The social network prohibits "any message that dehumanizes a group of people based on their religion, caste, age, disability, illness, national origin, race or ethnicity."
There hasn't been any embassy tweet since January 8. In order to recover your account, the embassy will need to delete that tweet. The diplomatic mission did not respond to messages asking for comment.
However, on Wednesday, a day after then-Secretary of State Mike Pompeo called Chinese actions "genocide," the Asian government called him an "apocalyptic clown" and called American policy "junk paper."
