The UN High Commissioner for Human Rights, the Chilean Michelle Bachelet, affirmed on Saturday that her visit to China was not "an investigation" and that her meetings in the Xinjiang region, where Beijing is accused of repressing minorities, were not supervised by the authorities.
Bachelet also assured at a press conference that she had listened to those who reproached her for her lack of criticism of the Chinese government and assured that she had spoken "frankly" to the communist leaders.
Beijing is accused of holding one million Uighurs and other Muslim minorities in detention centers in Xinjiang (northwest), of sterilizing women and forcing these citizens into forced labor.
The 70-year-old former Chilean president arrived in Xinjiang on Tuesday, the first visit by a senior U.N. human rights official in 17 years. Her aides said she was to visit the cities of Urumqi, the regional capital, and Kashgar.
At the end of her visit, in a telematic press conference, Bachelet urged China to avoid "arbitrary measures" in the "anti-terrorist" campaign that the authorities are carrying out in the Xinjiang region (northwest).
She assured that the authorities of this Chinese region had assured her that the network of "vocational training centers", which humanitarian organizations describe as forced re-education camps, had been "dismantled".
Bachelet also explained that she had mentioned the issue of the separation of Uyghur families, during her meetings with Xinjiang authorities.
"We are aware of the number of people who are looking for news of their loved ones. This and other issues were mentioned before the authorities," he said.
The United States and parliaments of other Western countries have denounced a "genocide", accusations vehemently denied by China, which claims that these are vocational training centers to keep the population away from separatism and extreme Islamism. (I)
