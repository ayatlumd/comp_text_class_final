In its great, epoch-defining strategic struggle against America, China believes it has a new weapon: identity politics. By weaponising the culture wars, China hopes to sap America's will, and its new approach was on display during last week's frosty diplomatic summit in Alaska.
At the tense sit-down in Anchorage, two minutes were allotted for the top diplomats to make opening remarks. The secretary of state, Antony Blinken, went first, rebuking China for its refusal to follow international norms.
Then came his counterpart, Yang Jiechi, who delivered a spectacularly awkward 18-minute rant about American perfidy. "There are many problems within the United States regarding human rights," he said, highlighting the Black Lives Matter campaign as evidence of America's moral turpitude.
This is part of a broader strategy. In recent months China has cottoned on to the fact that wokeness, America's new progressive religion, is so steeped in selfabasement that it might actually prove a useful tool in its race to surpass the creaking western superpower.
Sure, the argument goes, China has an authoritarian government that is ethnically cleansing Tibet and Xinjiang. Yes, it is dismantling the rule of law in
Hong Kong and might invade Taiwan in the coming years, uniting all Chinese people under the umbrella of its sinister surveillance state.
Yet China's hope is that by referencing America's problems with racial injustice, it can create an equivalence: you enslaved Africans and ethnically cleansed Native Americans; we're doing the same in Tibet and Xinjiang. You're as bad as us and deep down you know it.
And, in fairness, if this were 1821 or even 1921, it might have a point. But America's struggles with equality today are orders of magnitude less severe than the Uighur re-education camps in Xinjiang, where a Muslim minority is being stripped of its culture, religion, children and basic humanity.
The US's new progressive religion is so steeped in self-abasement that it might actually prove a useful tool
Never one to miss out on a chance to kick the Yanks, the righteous Vladimir Putin has jumped on the bandwagon too. When asked last week about his row with Joe Biden, in which the US president described him as a "killer", Putin responded by pointing to America's legacy of racism.
This is no coincidence. In the great power politics of the 21st century, Russia and China sense an American weakness. They understand that progressive elites in the US are fundamentally insular and aren't able properly to contextualise their own country's shortcomings.
There is a word for what China is doing: "wokewashing" - using American culture wars as a smokescreen for its own brutality. This was the thinking behind a bizarre tweet from the Chinese embassy in Washington's account, which claimed that "in the process of eradicating extremism, the minds of Uighur women in Xinjiang were emancipated, and gender equality and reproductive health were promoted, making them no longer baby-making machines. They are more confident and independent."
I'm sure these forcibly sterilised and imprisoned women are thoroughly enjoying their new independence.
Fortunately, the confused depravity of this tweet highlights the weakness of China's gambit. For now, at least, its diplomacy remains crass and cack-handed, its agenda painfully transparent China will continue to gamble on the hope that America's morally preening, self-loathing wokesters can't tell the difference between genocide in Xinjiang and inequality in Chicago. They really think American elites are that foolish and self-involved. And I fear they might be right.
