Twitter has locked the account of the Chinese embassy in Washington for a tweet that said that Uighur women had been emancipated and were “no longer baby-making machines”.
There have been no updates on the embassy’s account since January 9, two days after the tweet that partly read: “In the process of eradicating extremism, the minds of Uygur women in Xinjiang were emancipated and gender equality and reproductive health were promoted, making them no longer baby-making machines.” Twitter has hidden the tweet, which the Chinese embassy will have to delete manually to unlock its account.
The former US secretary of state, Mike Pompeo, had previously accused China of committing “crimes against humanity” and “genocide” against the Muslim Uighur population of Xinjiang. His nominated successor, Antony Blinken, said that he agreed with the assessment.
China has been accused by multiple human rights groups and governments, including the UK, of abusing the Uighur population; up to a million Uighurs have been put into labour camps for “re-education” and Uighur women have been forced to undergo sterilisation or be fitted with contraceptive devices.
Twitter said that the tweet by the Chinese embassy, which had cited a report by the state-run 
                   China Daily                    
newspaper, violated its “policy against dehumanisation . . . of a group of people based on their religion, caste, age, disability, serious disease, national origin, race, or ethnicity”.
China’s foreign ministry said that it found Twitter’s move “baffling” and said that China was a “major victim” of conspiracy theories and disinformation on social media.
Hua Chunying, a spokeswoman, said: “As there is so much ugly disinformation on China’s Xinjiang, the Chinese embassy in the US naturally has the responsibility and obligation to clarify things and present the truth.”
Even though China blocks access to Twitter within its borders, Chinese diplomats, embassies and media regularly use the platform to push their messages.
In June Twitter removed more than 170,000 accounts it said were linked to the Chinese government and pushing false information favourable to Beijing. China maintains that it is fighting separatism and extremism in the Xinjiang region.
