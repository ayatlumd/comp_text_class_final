CHINA is "not a cow" to be milked in her prime and "put to slaughter in the end", according to the country's deputy ambassador, who has denied Beijing has done anything "intentionally" to hurt Australia.
Despite trade sanctions on major Australian exports including barley, wine and coal being imposed last year, the embassy's deputy head of mission, Wang Xining, claimed China was actually the injured party.
Speaking at the National Press Club on Wednesday, Mr Wang said Australia's decision to block Huawei's 5G bid was an example of the US using a "state power" to suppress a Chinese company.
"We have done nothing intentionally to hurt this relationship and we have seen too many incidents over the past few years that China's interest has been hurt," he said.
Mr Wang said in the Chinese lunar calendar it was the Year of the Ox, and while his country often saw themselves as "hardworking cattle - like an ox", they were "not a cow".
"I don't think anybody should fancy the idea to milk China when she's in her prime and plot to slaughter it in the end," he said.
This week a new Human Rights Watch report accused China of "crimes against humanity" for its treatment of Uighurs in Xinjiang, an allegation Mr Wang denied.
The discussion was hosted by club president and ABC 7.30 reporter Laura Tingle, who was greeted by Mr Wang with a kiss on the hand.
But he was less enthusiastic when Ms Tingle asked him why a Uighur woman who was also an Australian citizen had been unable to speak to her husband who had been jailed for 25 years in Xinjiang for no clear reason.
Mr Wang instead said the "whole Chinese nation" cared about the welfare of Uighurs.
"We want every ethnic group to thrive, to live a good life, and we know how to deal with it well," he said.
Despite this assertion, the Human Rights Watch reported detailed cases of Chinese authorities' alleged torture and rape of Uighur women in Xinjiang.
They claimed about a million people were detained in what the Chinese government claims are "re-education camps".
Australia and other countries have been urged to step up sanctions as more evidence of abuse emerges.
