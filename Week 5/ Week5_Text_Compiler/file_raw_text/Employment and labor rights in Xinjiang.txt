There are 56 ethnic groups in total in the People's Republic of China (PRC), all of which enjoy equal rights. The State defends and develops relations of equality, unity and mutual aid. Discrimination and exclusion of any of them is prohibited.
The Constitution of the PRC establishes ethnic equality and national unity as the starting point in the approach to the issue of minorities. In accordance with the peculiarities and needs of each national minority, the State assists national minority areas to accelerate their economic and cultural development.
In addition, the Constitution stipulates that all citizens have the right and obligation to work. China is committed to the philosophy of human-centered development, attaches great importance to job security and high priority to employment, and implements a proactive set of employment policies.
Xinjiang launched a three-year program to intensify its poverty alleviation efforts in 22 provinces in the south and four extremely poor regiment farms under the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (CPCX).
Xinjiang provides dynamic, categorized and targeted assistance to people struggling to find work and unemployed households throughout the autonomous region, identifying, registering, assisting and guaranteeing stable employment for each and every one of them.
China's Criminal Law, Labor Law, Labor Contract Law and Public Security Administrative Penalty Law stipulate that forcing a person to work by violence, threat or illegal limitation of personal freedom; or insulting, physically punishing, beating, illegally searching or detaining an employee, are strictly prohibited and will result in administrative penalties.
Xinjiang strictly implements relevant state regulations and adopts the eight-hour workday and 40-hour workweek system.
To guarantee women's rights, Xinjiang strives to remove barriers to employment and formulates policies to help women start their own businesses. The Chinese government is committed to respecting citizens' right to work, safeguarding their legitimate labor rights and interests, and guaranteeing them decent employment. Strictly following the above principles enshrined in the PRC Constitution and relevant national laws, such as the Labor Law, Labor Contract Law, Employment Promotion Law, Social Security Law, Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests and Law on the Protection of Persons with Disabilities, Xinjiang has formulated and implemented a series of autonomous regional regulations based on local conditions, including measures to implement the Employment Promotion Law, the Regulations on Labor and Social Security Supervision, the Law on the Protection of Women's Rights and Interests and the Law on the Protection of Persons with Disabilities, as well as the Regulations of the Xinjiang Uygur Autonomous Region on the Protection of Workers' Rights and Interests.
China prohibits child labor, opposes forced labor, labor discrimination and sexual harassment in the workplace, takes specific measures to combat illegal employment, and prevents and punishes all kinds of violations.
In the United Nations 2030 Agenda for Sustainable Development, "end poverty in all its forms everywhere" tops all other development goals, expressing a pressing demand from the international community in this regard.
Chinese President Xi Jinping, also general secretary of the CPC Central Committee and chairman of the Central Military Commission, emphasized that employment is fundamental to people's well-being.
The proactive policies to ensure employment and job security adopted by the local government of Xinjiang have effectively protected the basic labor rights of all ethnic groups, greatly improved their living and working conditions, and fully satisfied their aspirations to create a better life.
