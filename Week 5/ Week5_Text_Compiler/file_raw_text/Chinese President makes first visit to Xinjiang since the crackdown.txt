China's President Xi Jinping paid a public visit to Xinjiang (northwest), state media reported Friday, the first since the crackdown began in this region where his government is accused of detaining more than a million Uighurs and other Muslim minorities in camps.
The state-run Xinhua news agency said Xi praised the progress made during an inspection visit to that region, the first since 2014, when three people were killed in an attack that presaged the start of a mass detention campaign three years later.
The United States calls China's actions in Xinjiang "genocide" and "crimes against humanity," accusations Beijing vehemently denies, saying its security measures are a necessary response to extremism.
Xi also praised the work of the Xinjiang Production and Construction Corps (XPCC), a paramilitary organization sanctioned by Washington for alleged human rights violations.
The group made "great progress" in reform and development, Xi said during a trip Wednesday to the northern city of Shihezi in the region, according to the Xinhua report.
State media published images showing Xi talking to students and local officials, taking part in a singing and dancing performance, and receiving enthusiastic applause from residents dressed in traditional clothing.
During her visit to China in May, UN High Commissioner for Human Rights Michelle Bachelet urged authorities to avoid "arbitrary and indiscriminate" measures in Xinjiang. But she noted that her visit was not "an investigation".
That tour was sharply criticized by human rights and Uighur groups abroad.
Beijing is accused of holding Uighurs and other Muslim minority people in detention centers in Xinjiang, sterilizing women and forcing these citizens into forced labor.
China denies all allegations and claims that the labor camps are vocational training centers aimed at fighting extremist Islamism.
