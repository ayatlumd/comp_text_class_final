The birthrate in the western Chinese region of Xinjiang, home to many of the country's repressed Uighur minority, has almost halved amid accusations of genocide.
Such an extreme decline is unprecedented in the 71 years that the UN has been collecting global fertility statistics, and is larger than the fall in the birthrate during the Syrian civil war and the genocides in Rwanda and Cambodia, according to Nathan Ruser, one of the authors of a report by the Australian Strategic Policy Institute, a think tank.
"Our analysis builds on previous work and provides compelling evidence that Chinese government policies in Xinjiang may constitute an act of genocide," the report said. It concluded that China was "cracking down on the reproductive rights" of Uighur and other mostly Muslim minorities in Xinjiang "over perceived fears of instability and uneven" population growth.
The research used the Chinese government's statistics to determine that birthrates in Xinjiang fell 49 per cent between 2017 and 2019. The largest declines were in counties where Uighurs and other largely Muslim minority communities are concentrated.
The authorities check Uighur and other minority women in Xinjiang to see if they are pregnant, and have forced them to fit intrauterine devices to prevent pregnancy and to undergo sterilisation and abortion, according to research by Adrian Zenz, a German anthropologist, and the Associated Press.
On Wednesday human rights groups and western nations led by Britain, the United States and Germany accused China of crimes against Uighurs and demanded that it allow UN experts into Xinjiang. China denies the accusations.
