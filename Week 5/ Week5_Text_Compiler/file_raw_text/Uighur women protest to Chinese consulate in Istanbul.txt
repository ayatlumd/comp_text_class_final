Several hundred Uighur women demonstrated Monday to China's consulate general in Istanbul to denounce Beijing's mistreatment of this Muslim minority.
"Stop the genocide!" and "Free our close friends!" shouted between 300 and 400 people, mostly women, who gathered near Chinese diplomatic representation, an AFP photographer said.
On International Women's Day, the protesters also denounced the sexual violence they said the Chinese authorities are exercising on Uighurs.
"Rape is a crime against humanity!" he said, "Give uighur women a voice!" the banners prayed.
In an investigation published last month, the BBC revealed allegations of systematic rape, sexual abuse and torture -- including violations with canes that produce electric shocks -- to women detained in Xinjiang, a region of western China where Uighurs are the main ethnic group.
This investigation, in addition to allegations of forced sterilization by various associations, provoked outrage from Western nations.
In addition to sexual violence, China is accused of holding more than one million Uighurs and members of other Muslim minorities in Xinjiang camps in detention.
Beijing denies these accusations and rejects the term "camps", claiming that they are vocational training centres, aimed at employing the population and moving them away from religious extremism.
