CHINA is "not a cow" to be milked in her prime and "put to slaughter in the end", says the country's deputy ambassador, as he denied Beijing had done anything "intentionally" to hurt Australia.
Despite slapping trade sanctions on major Australian exports including barley, wine and coal last year, the Chinese embassy's deputy head of mission, Wang Xining, claimed yesterday China was the injured party.
Speaking at the National Press Club, Mr Wang said Australia's decision to block Huawei's 5G bid was an example of the US using a "state power" to suppress a Chinese company.
"We have done nothing intentionally to hurt this relationship, and we have seen too many incidents over the past few years that China's interest has been hurt," he said.
Mr Yang said in the Chinese lunar calendar it was the Year of the Ox, and while his country often saw themselves as "hardworking cattle, like an ox", they were "not a cow".
"I don't think anybody should fancy the idea to milk China when she's in her prime and plot to slaughter it in the end," he said. This week, a new Human Rights Watch report accused China of "crimes against humanity" for its treatment of Uyghurs in Xinjiang, an allegation Mr Wang denied.
The discussion was hosted by National Press Club president and ABC 7.30 political reporter Laura Tingle, who was greeted by Mr Wang with a kiss on the hand.
But he was less enthusiastic when Tingle asked him why a Uyghur woman, who was also an Australian citizen, had been unable to speak to her husband who had been jailed for 25 years in Xinjiang for no clear reason.
Mr Wang said the "whole Chinese nation" cared about the welfare of Uyghurs. "We want every ethnic group to thrive, to live a good life, and we know how to deal with it well," he said.
Despite this assertion, the Human Rights Watch report detailed horrific cases of Chinese authorities' alleged torture and rape of Uyghur women in Xinjiang. They claimed about one million people were detained in what the Chinese Government said were "re-education camps".
