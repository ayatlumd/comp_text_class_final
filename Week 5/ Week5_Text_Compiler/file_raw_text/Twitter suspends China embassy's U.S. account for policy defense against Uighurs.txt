Twitter reported on Thursday (21) that blocked the account of the Chinese embassy in the United States, after a tweet stating that Uighur women were no longer "machines to make babies" after their minds were "emancipated".

A Twitter spokesperson told AFP news agency that it took action against the publication for "violating our policy against dehumanization."

On January 7, the Chinese embassy account published that Uighur women "were emancipated" and were no longer "baby-making machines," citing a study reported by the state-run China Daily newspaper —the text said that "the eradication of extremism has given Xinjiang women more autonomy" on the subject of having children.

The post was removed by Twitter after a wave of criticism on the internet. The suspension of the bill, however, came a day after the Trump administration, in its final hours, accused China of committing genocide in Xinjiang -- a view shared by Joe Biden's administration.

The indictment does not generate any automatic penalty, but puts pressure on countries and companies to stop doing business in the region, one of the world's largest suppliers of cotton.

China is internationally criticized for keeping Uighurs, a majority-Muslim minority, in huge detention centers in the Xinjiang region.

In 2018, a UN team received complaints that at least 1 million Uighurs and other Muslim minorities were detained. Another study, released in September 2020, points to the existence of 380 detention camps.

Beijing denies the allegations of abuse and says the sites are re-education spaces aimed at fighting extremism and teaching new skills.

Uighurs have faced a comprehensive campaign for three years to turn them into followers of the Communist Party, weaken their commitment to Islam, and transfer them from farms to factories.

Although Twitter is blocked in China, it has been adopted by state media and diplomats who use the platform to defend Beijing's positions.

Chinese Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Hua Chunying said Thursday that she was confused by Twitter's action.

"There are numerous reports and information related to Xinjiang that are against China. It is the responsibility of our embassy in the U.S. to clarify the truth," he said.
