Twitter reported Thursday to have blocked the Chinese embassy account in the United States, following a tweet claiming that Uighur women were no longer "baby-making machines" after their minds had been "emancipated."
A Twitter spokesman told the AFP that he took action against the tweet for "violating our policy against dehumanization."
More than one million Uighurs and other minorities, mostly Muslims, are believed to be or were held in re-education camps in the northwestern region of Xinjiang, where China is also accused of forcilly sterilizing women.
China denies the allegations and asserts that facilities in the region are not camps, but job training centres to keep people away from terrorism.
On January 7, China's U.S. embassy tweeted that "in the process of eradicating extremism," Uighur women's minds "emancipated and gender equality and reproductive health were promoted, making them no longer baby-making machines."
The tweet, which has since been hidden - after a wave of online protests - included a link to a China Daily state media article that stated that "the eradication of extremism has given Xinjiang women more autonomy" on the issue of having children.
Twitter's policy prohibits "the dehumanization of a group of people based on their religion, caste, age, disability, serious illness, national origin, race or ethnicity," the social network states, adding that the embassy account is temporarily blocked until the tweet is removed, considered a violation of this policy.
