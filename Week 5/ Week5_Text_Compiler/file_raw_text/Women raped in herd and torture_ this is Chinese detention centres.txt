Choking deaths, chained students and women raped in herd by security guards. These are some of the sufferings to which members of Muslim minorities are subjected in Xinjiang, China.
A CNN news team, led by journalists Ivan Watson and Rebecca Wright, gathered testimonies from women who account for old age. 
According to the U.S. chain's record, the most abominable human rights violations in the Chinese-controlled region have been encouraged to point out, for fear of economic retaliation by the Asian giant.
The Uighur minority is a victim of state violence, while China defends itself by responding that these are centers of "re-education."
"There is no systematic sexual assault or abuse against women in Xinjiang," the Chinese government said in response to CNN. 
However, the courageous testimonies of women who managed to get out of these centres alive confirm the horrendous abuses.
The horror of Xinjiang
Qelbinur Sidik taught children in a school. One day, without much explanation, she was forging to teach Mandarin classes to the detainees in Xinjiang for three months. 
Learning the language is one of the conditions that the authorities impose on detainees.
Sidik began his obligatory work in March 2017. She went from caring for children between the age of six and thirteen to older adults. 
When he first saw them, he couldn't believe it: they were all chained, feet and hands, as if they were high-danger criminals. 
The teacher recounted some of the horror to CNN: "When the guards drank at night, the cops told each other how they raped and tortured the girls." 
Sidik saw with her own eyes how a woman died as a result of these herd rapes. "There was no spark of life on his face. His cheeks were colorless, he wasn't breathing," he recalled. 
The victim was carried by other guards and a policewoman confirmed to her what had happened: torture, rape and the consequent murder of the woman.
Torture
The transformation of the detainees was evident over the days. 
The "students in the process of re-education" who first arrived at the center where she worked looked healthy, in good physical condition and well fed. 
However, within a few days, it was all skin and bones. 
Many times, when he turned around to write on the board, he listened to those present cry in shame. 
He also heard other sounds, more guttural and strange, coming from other rooms in the building. However, it was not the music in a classroom, but the disturbing noise of torture.
After the traumatic experience, Sidik decided to emigrate from China. He now lives with his family in Holland.
"I was raped as a group"
Tursunay Ziyawudun is one of Xinjiang's victims. She was arrested in April 2017, although she had committed no crime. 
She was taken to the "County Vocational School ofXinyuan," in Xinjiang. "Vocational" is the euphemism that the regime uses to soften the true identity of its detention camps. 
There she was a month in prison, but a year later she was again apprehended.
During the day I received some food and water. To the bathroom, he said, he could go for up to five minutes a day. If he went too far, he could suffer consequences. 
"Those who spent the most time received electric shocks with canes," he said.
The interrogations, strange to be an alleged vocational school, were endless. 
Ziyawudun said two female guards took her to another room where she was placed on a table. "They inserted a paralyzing cane inside me and twisted me and electrocuted me with it. I fainted."
Ten days later, she was taken out of her cell and taken somewhere else. "I heard another girl crying and screaming. I saw about five or six men entering that room. I thought she was being tortured. But then I was raped as a group," the woman said.
"After that I realized what they had also done to her. They were extremely sadistic, causing pain and damage to the body by hitting and hitting my head against the wall... was his way of punishing us," said the victim who lives in America today.
His journey was long. When she was released, in September 2019, the regime warned her: she could not talk to anyone about what she had experienced in that "vocational" center.
